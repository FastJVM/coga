## Switching value of the two central ideas — 2026-09-13

The owner asks whether [chosen delegation and understandable ownership](../contexts/marketing/positioning/SKILL.md)
are compelling reasons to switch. **Together they give Coga a strong
potential adoption motive for a particular user: delegate a job on your
terms, with the means of directing it in a form you can read and change.**
They give a satisfied user of an equally adaptable setup little immediate
reason to leave. This is a judgment about motivation, informed by the
product comparisons; conversion and preference have not been measured.

| Idea | Value to the prospective user | Strength as a reason to change tools |
|---|---|---|
| Choose the level and manner of delegation | Specify a step, delegate an outcome, or request investigation while retaining the decision. Keep that mandate explicit and revise it as needed. | **Potentially strong** when the current way of directing agents makes this awkward. Outcome prompts and detailed instructions already work in other agents; the gain must come from how Coga lets the person manage the arrangement. |
| Own a system you understand and can change | Locate the brief, selected knowledge, method and recorded progress; change those materials or extend the tooling. Agents can help with the edits. | **Strong for people who actively shape their tools; modest for people already content with theirs.** An open-source license alone does not demonstrate practical understandability. Show where a useful change is made. |
| Both together | Delegate meaningful responsibility while retaining a practical way to inspect and reshape how the work is directed. | **The strongest combined promise.** It connects the desired work outcome to the operator's ability to intervene. A single one-off job can demonstrate it. |

Illustrations of the first principle include “make this change using these
constraints,” “investigate and recommend; I decide,” and “deliver this
outcome; choose the method.” The product explanation should show the
editable task, context and chosen handoffs behind one such mandate. The
[source review](pitch-evaluation.md#closest-comparisons-for-delegating-one-job)
grounds those mechanisms. Legibility concerns Coga's directing material,
tooling and recorded state; it does not reveal a model's internal reasoning.

### Who has a reason to move?

| Current setup | Switching judgment for these two ideas |
|---|---|
| Direct agent use, with work mostly directed in conversations | **Most promising reason to add Coga** when the person wants the brief, selected context and handoffs to become a maintained work record. A brief and an ordinary agent session may already satisfy a small job. Show the additional value on work they care about. |
| [Zed](https://zed.dev/docs/ai/agent-panel) | **Conditional.** Editable prompts, context selection and saved threads already exist. Coga may suit someone who wants tasks, methods and knowledge in repository files as their continuing interface. The prospective user's stated need for surviving restarts is already served; these principles alone give that person no demonstrated reason to switch. Zed can remain their editor. |
| [Superset](https://docs.superset.sh/tasks) | **Conditional.** Editable tasks and configurable task-to-agent prompts already overlap. Coga needs to make maintaining the mandate, method, context and handoffs in its files preferable for that user's work. “Control your agent” by itself will not explain the gain. |
| [Backlog.md](https://github.com/MrLesk/Backlog.md) | **Weak on ownership alone; potentially meaningful for execution.** Local Markdown tasks are already central. The reason to adopt is Coga's supplied composition, launch, task stages and upkeep when those solve a remaining problem. |
| [Pi](https://pi.dev/) | **Weak as a replacement argument based on these principles.** Pi explicitly centers ownership, workflow customization and prompt/context control. Coga offers a supplied task and knowledge method around agent CLIs. Its value depends on wanting that arrangement; this is not a tested Coga/Pi integration. |
| [Compound Engineering](https://github.com/EveryInc/compound-engineering-plugin/blob/main/skills/ce-work/SKILL.md) | **Weak as a broad switching argument.** Editable work definitions, one-off execution and the knowledge loop already overlap, including non-code work. The [continuity review](continuity-comparison.md) identifies Coga's concrete defaults for context delivery, human stages and reviewed instruction upkeep. Those specific preferences can motivate adoption; the two ideals alone do not establish a better experience. |

### Consequence for the pitch

Lead with the work the person can entrust to an agent, then show that the
means of directing it are understandable and changeable. “Control,”
“ownership” and “hackability” need that connection to a useful job to become
a reason to adopt. For a person who wants to shape how agents work, this
can be a significant choice of working method even where the underlying
capabilities are available elsewhere.

Coga launches configured existing agent CLIs, so the first adoption can be
one task using an existing agent. The person need not replace an editor or
migrate all work to evaluate that experience. This is the most plausible
entry route; reusable methods and Dream can become reasons to keep using
it. No measured setup, review-time or outcome advantage is claimed, and
human review is not assumed to be an extra cost unique to Coga. Keep the
endorsed pitch and explanation while developing this concrete reason to try.

## Worked comparison: asynchronous delegation — 2026-09-13

**September 14 synthesis:** the owner's
[message hierarchy](../contexts/marketing/positioning/SKILL.md)
uses megalaunch to demonstrate the immediate payoff and the editable
working method to explain the product's depth. Show the queue progressing
around blockers, then how the operator defines and changes that delegation.

The owner requested the concrete case behind “the task/context/workflow
interface suits their work better,” then identified megalaunch and
asynchronous work as a possible sufficient reason to switch.

**Megalaunch makes a stronger case than a preference for Markdown.** Prepare
jobs when you have the context to define them, let eligible work advance,
and handle the recorded decisions later. The task files hold the assignment
and the current division of responsibility throughout. This is a substantial
addition to the documented Zed thread interaction for someone wanting a
prepared work queue. Superset already supplies an asynchronous working
rhythm, so “Coga is async; both alternatives are synchronous” does not hold.

**Owner's correction:** the key behavior is **block the task, end its agent
session, and move to the next eligible job**. A missing human answer releases
execution to other prepared work. The previous comparison with background
notifications understated this policy: a notification alone does not
establish automatic progression through a task queue.

### A concrete one-off job, then a queue

**Authored illustration, not a record of a product trial:** prepare a product
announcement. The agent proposes three angles from the supplied release
facts; the human chooses an angle; the agent drafts the announcement using
the recorded choice and house style. Nothing is published by this example.

In Coga, the task holds the goal, constraints and current notes. Its
`contexts:` selects the release facts and house style. A short authored
workflow assigns proposal to the agent, choice to the human, and drafting
to the agent. A person wanting to delegate the angle too can instead author
one agent step for the whole draft. Both are one-off jobs; recurring
automation is unnecessary.

Now prepare two other independent jobs: check documentation links and
summarize some supplied interview notes. A possible queue result is:

| Job | What the agent can do | What remains for the human |
|---|---|---|
| Announcement | Produce the proposed angles; reach the human workflow step | Choose the angle in the task's working notes |
| Documentation check | Complete the requested report | Read the result when useful |
| Interview summary | Discover that the named attachment is missing and record a blocker | Supply the attachment |

Megalaunch's [engine](../../src/coga/megalaunch.py) services eligible tasks and
chains agent steps until that job reaches a handoff, blocker or terminal
state. When the agent records `coga block`, the session ends, the blocked
outcome returns to the sweep, and the sweep attempts the next eligible job
within its configured limits. The question remains with the parked task.
The
[queue instructions](../../src/coga/resources/prompt-megalaunch.md) tell the
agent to record unavailable input as a blocker rather than leave the queue
waiting for a chat answer. This instruction depends on agent compliance;
the runtime advances when the lifecycle transition is recorded.

After the human records the angle and advances the task to its next agent
stage, the next announcement launch can use an edited brief, corrected
release facts and the saved choice together. The
[composer](../../src/coga/compose.py) reads selected context, current-step
instructions and the task's working notes from disk. The earlier
[isolated checks](../archive/launch-programs/phase-0-audit/source-inspection-results.json)
verified those composition and handoff mechanisms. Edits affect the next
composition; already running sessions do not receive them automatically.
An existing ticket's step sequence is frozen, so changing a workflow
template's routing alone does not rewrite that ticket's assignments.

The useful change for the operator is concrete: prepare several assignments
once, return to completed work and explicit decisions, and edit the material
that will direct subsequent work. Reading and changing a file is also an
operation the agent can perform on the operator's behalf.

### Do the same jobs in Zed and Superset

| Operation | Zed's documented path | Superset's documented path | Coga's supplied arrangement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Define the announcement | Write a request, attach the brief/facts, invoke a skill | Edit a task, supply context, launch with a configurable prompt template | Author the task and attach named context; choose its workflow |
| Work while the human is elsewhere | Independent threads run in the background; notifications report completion | Agents run in workspaces; status and notifications expose finished or waiting work | Megalaunch works through the eligible ticket queue |
| Leave the angle decision to the human | Ask the agent to stop at the decision; answer in the thread or a maintained file | Agent pauses for input; workspace is flagged; a coordinator can handle other work | Task advances to its human-assigned stage; the sweep can service other tasks |
| Resume from an edited assignment | Update the files/message and continue or start a thread with that material | Edit the task/files and continue or relaunch the workspace agent | Launch the saved current task stage; composition reads its current material |

Zed supports explicit file context, editable messages, background
notifications and saved threads. Its instruction files and skills can
also implement a file-directed method. The additional Coga benefit is the
supplied queue over independently staged tasks, with responsibility and
selected context maintained alongside each job. It is a reason to add Coga
when directing a collection of jobs matters; Zed can remain the editor.
[Agent Panel](https://zed.dev/docs/ai/agent-panel),
[instructions and skills](https://zed.dev/docs/ai/instructions).

Superset's own stated rhythm is to dispatch work, leave the application in
the background and return when notified. Its coordinator can distribute
assignments and collect outcomes; scheduled audits explicitly leave results
and human decisions for later review. These establish asynchronous use,
but do not by themselves establish the corrected queue behavior. Its CLI
and agents can also maintain custom repository files.
[Task launch](https://docs.superset.sh/tasks),
[status and notifications](https://docs.superset.sh/agent-status),
[orchestration](https://docs.superset.sh/orchestration),
[overnight audit](https://docs.superset.sh/recipes/nightly-audit).

The subsequent [orchestration-protocol inspection](https://github.com/superset-sh/skills/blob/main/skills/superset-orchestrate/SKILL.md)
finds a concrete distinction: Superset's coordinator keeps dependency and
completion state in its working context. Worker completion/blocker markers
are prompt conventions read from terminal output, not durable Superset
events. The coordinator interprets those reports and schedules work. This
supports related coordination, but does not establish the same runtime
queue over durable task states.

In Coga, the blocker is written into the task and the runtime moves on.
Both arrangements rely on an agent reporting its blocker. Coga supplies
the transition and subsequent queue progression in code, with an inspectable
task as its input. That makes the task/context/workflow distinction
operational: the files are what the dispatcher actually acts on, as well
as what the human and agent edit.

**Switching verdict:** the prepared task queue is a concrete, potentially
substantial gain over ordinary Zed use. Against Superset, there is now a
more specific case than file-interface preference: Coga supplies durable
task transitions and automatic queue progression when a job blocks.
This is a defensible architectural advantage for someone wanting that
behavior supplied by the runtime. It does not establish better overall
results, nor imply that Superset lacks autonomous coordination. Compare
that exact behavior when assessing a replacement.

Megalaunch services jobs sequentially through terminal sessions. “Async”
here describes when the person defines work and handles decisions. It does
not mean concurrent execution or a headless background service. Its default
dependency pass can retry blockers naming finished tasks; it is not a
general dependency-graph scheduler. These details preserve the promise:
work can progress without requiring an immediate human conversation at
every step, while the assignments and return points remain inspectable.

## AGI-ready proposal and claim check

**September 12, 2026 — owner proposal:** “The only AGI-ready framework.”
The rationale is broad delegation to agents, visible working material and
human choice over how much freedom to give them. This is a new candidate,
not selected campaign copy.

**Owner's AGI clarification:** the reason for this framing is that the need
to direct an agent remains even with hypothetical AGI. Capability alone
does not specify whose objectives to pursue, which priorities and tradeoffs
matter, or what authority has been delegated. An agent can help formulate
those choices; the operator still needs a way to express and revise the
mandate. The thesis concerns an enduring need for direction, beyond adapting
the product to successive model upgrades.

The underlying thesis is **the way of working evolves with agent capability,
while the need to direct that work remains**. More capable agents may need
less procedural detail, allowing direction at the level of broader outcomes,
constraints and responsibilities. Tasks, context and workflows keep that
direction explicit and editable in Coga.
Coga's implementation gives that thesis concrete objects:

- Agents can help prepare tasks, execute them, coordinate eligible work and
  propose maintenance of shared instructions. Delegation extends to operating
  the work system itself.
- Briefs, context, workflow instructions, current responsibility and blockers
  remain in inspectable files. The human can revise the material that directs
  subsequent work; chat and agents can help with those revisions.
- Workflow assignments specify human return points. Dream proposes changes
  to shared guidance for human acceptance. Responsibility can be chosen when
  defining the workflow, without requiring the human to perform every step.

[The source inspection](usage-comparison.md#source-code-distinction-from-langgraph-and-crewai)
supports these present mechanisms. Their value with future models is a design
bet. “Degree of freedom” is expressed through task scope, instructions, step
assignments and review policy. Coga enforces parts of task routing; conduct
instructions and the underlying agent's permissions also govern execution.
This is not a tested guarantee of control over hypothetical AGI.

**Exclusivity and wording check:** Kortix's July 17, 2026
[architecture article](https://kortix.com/blog/agi-ready-architecture) already
uses “AGI-ready” for stronger agents under retained human authority. It also
claims Git-backed operating files and reviewed learning. This establishes
direct positioning overlap; its implementation has not been inspected here.
Add it as a comparator to verify before asserting architectural exclusivity,
without treating its published claims as demonstrated equivalence.

**Launch-pitch evaluation:** editorial judgment, not audience testing:
**8/10 for coherence of the design thesis; 4/10 for the exact headline.**
These assess the proposal, not Coga's overall competitive differentiation.

The strong idea is an editable division of work. A human can initially
define a detailed task and delegate execution; a different workflow can
also delegate preparation and proposals to improve shared instructions,
while preserving chosen human decisions. The source inspection supports
those mechanisms. Whether stronger future agents make them more effective
remains a hypothesis.

The headline has ambition, but it leaves the product and today's benefit
unclear. For the planned Bookface launch, the editorial expectation is that
“framework” suggests something the reader must build with, and “AGI-ready”
invites a debate about future capability before explaining useful work now.
Neither audience reaction has been tested. The wording claims uniqueness
without communicating the mechanism; removing “the only” corrects the
unsupported assertion but leaves the clarity problem.

The launch message should make the editable division of work concrete:
agents can prepare, execute and propose changes to the method; the operator
chooses the responsibilities and accepted instructions. Show the same work
moving between those roles through visible tasks, context and workflows.
AGI readiness can frame the longer-term ambition, but the present benefit
must carry the reason to try Coga.

**Originality of the moving frontier:** the owner asks whether this is the
key originality. It is a candidate organizing principle and user benefit;
adjustable delegation itself is already shared territory. For example,
[VS Code custom agents](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/agent-customization/custom-agents)
have editable Markdown instructions, tool selections and human-initiated
handoffs, and an agent can generate those definitions. LangGraph supports
configurable human intervention and mixed deterministic/agentic execution
in its [application runtime](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/overview).
These precedents constrain the broad claim without establishing identical
operating methods.

Coga's concrete design contribution is the relationship established in the
source inspection: work definitions, saved human/agent responsibilities and
the procedure for maintaining shared instructions are ordinary material in
one task system. Humans and existing agent CLIs operate on those files and
commands. This connects the Markdown interface, workflows, external agents
and Dream into a coherent method. The moving frontier describes a benefit
of that method; it does not establish a new exclusive capability.

**Owner's subsequent clarification:** the moving frontier means the whole
way of working changes as model capabilities improve. The assistant had
narrowed this to reassigning stages within an existing workflow. The broader
claim includes changing the size of tasks, how much planning is prescribed,
which stages are useful, where human judgment is needed and how the working
method itself is maintained.

Coga's design bet is **the method remains editable as the capabilities of
the people and agents operating it change**. Humans and agents can help
revise tasks, context and workflows; human acceptance determines which
proposed improvements become shared guidance. A stronger agent can make a
different procedure practical, and the operator can adopt that procedure
through changes to the material that already directs work. This is the
connection between the owner's AGI-ready ambition and the Markdown entry
point, workflow authoring and Dream loop.

The implementation supports revising that material; benefits from future
capability gains remain a hypothesis. Coga does not automatically redesign
workflows on a model upgrade, and changing a workflow definition does not
rewrite existing frozen step sequences. The delegation precedents above
address only part of this broader thesis; the existing method/upkeep
comparisons also matter. Evaluate the complete adaptation experience before
claiming originality or superiority. The implementation overlap with Kortix
remains open.

### The common message and the demonstration

execute**. The written brief describes this job; shared context supplies
accepted knowledge; the workflow describes how the job moves between people

deliberately. The relative emphasis changes with the audience: task authority
for someone coordinating conversations, execution and upkeep for someone

an extensible agent or automation system.

proposed wording, not a claim that every run improves performance.

A useful launch illustration would follow a feature fix, a market study and

subsequent tickets; existing tickets retain their saved step sequence.

### Prompt control and hackability

The owner's follow-up distinguishes managing the assembled work prompt
from allowing an editable task description, and adds a tool that is legible
enough to use and extend. That is a more specific criterion than the presence
of a prompt editor. Coga assembles the supported task sections, selected

prompt. The assembly is explicit Python in [compose.py](../../src/coga/compose.py),
with the layers documented in [architecture](../contexts/coga/architecture/SKILL.md).

The control has a boundary: the base prompt and session-conduct layers are
package resources, not project-local overrides. Changing those layers today
is a package/source change. The agent host contributes instructions and
behavior beyond Coga's composed input. “Coga manages the work prompt” is
supported; “every part of the model's prompt is editable in the project” is
not. This also limits broader historical wording in the principles and vision.

Coga's concrete extension surface is familiar: Markdown workflows and skills
can define a different process; contexts can add shared knowledge; ordinary
scripts can perform deterministic work, with `ticket.py` reserved for a
ticket's headless phase. Deeper changes live in the Python runtime. These are
structural reasons to call the tool hackable. They do not establish that a
new user finds it easier to understand than alternatives.
[Source layout and extension boundary](../contexts/coga/codebase/SKILL.md).

The competitors are not uniformly closed around a user-editable message.

assembly, editable methods and extensibility therefore have real precedents.

The marketing opportunity is the experience of operating and adapting Coga
through these familiar materials. “The prompt is the interface. The tool is
yours to change” joins the interaction model to that purpose. It is candidate
positioning for operators who want to shape their tools, not a claim that
other tools cannot be changed or a demonstrated reason to migrate.

## The adoption case

Coga's strongest case is a person who repeatedly prepares work for agents,
maintains instructions across that work, and needs ongoing human/agent
handoffs. The task and context files govern what runs; workflow state records
where it stands; megalaunch services eligible tickets; Dream/Retro propose
changes to shared knowledge for human merge. AI can draft, select, summarize
and maintain this material. Direct file control is available without requiring
the human to write every document manually.

The intended benefit is less repeated explanation and coordination. Neither
the Markdown extension, agent-written documents, parallel agents nor persistent
workspaces alone establishes an advantage. The [upkeep audit](upkeep-audit.md)
demonstrates automated maintenance while also identifying a failed reuse of
accepted knowledge. Coga must make the complete loop useful enough to repay
any additional setup and upkeep compared with the existing workflow, while
also counting explanation, supervision and correction that it removes.
Setup and review that would happen anyway are not a differential cost of
Coga. Neither extra nor reduced total human attention has been demonstrated;
automatic improvement is not established.

## Where the adoption argument is strongest

The clearest functional addition is for a Markdown task user who currently
assembles execution and knowledge upkeep themselves. For CE and Spec Kit
